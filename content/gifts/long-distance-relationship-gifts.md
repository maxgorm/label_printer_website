A good long-distance gift does one simple job: it puts you in the room when you can't be there. That might be a letter on their nightstand, a clock showing your time, or a hoodie that still smells like you.

So we've grouped these 30 ideas by what they do, not by price. Some are keepsakes, some show up every day, and some are just plans for the next visit. Quick note: this guide is written by the Sentimo team, and we make one of the products mentioned.

> **The short version**
>
> - People tend to value physical things more than digital ones, which hints at why a letter or print carries weight (Atasoy and Morewedge, 2018).
> - Small, unexpected reach-outs matter. Research on friends and acquaintances (Liu et al., 2022) found people underestimate how much a surprise reach-out is appreciated.
> - Price isn't the point. In one study (Flynn and Adams, 2009), recipients didn't appreciate pricier birthday gifts more.
> - Shipping abroad? Check both USPS rules and the destination country's rules before you pack.

## Keepsakes they can hold

Texts scroll away. A keepsake stays on the shelf, in a drawer, or tucked inside a book. Researchers Atasoy and Morewedge found that across five experiments, people valued [physical versions of goods more than digital ones](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v44y2018i6p1343-1357..html), including souvenir photos and books. They linked this to a stronger sense of ownership. That study measured value to the owner, not a gift recipient's feelings, but it's a good hint about why paper sticks.

### 1. Open-when letters

Write a stack of sealed envelopes labeled for different moments: "open when you can't sleep," "open when you get good news," "open on our anniversary." They're cheap, personal, and they keep working for months.

### 2. A small printed photo album

Pick a handful of favorite photos from your time together and print them. Add a line under each one about what was happening just outside the frame.

### 3. A journal you mail back and forth

One of you writes a few pages, then posts it. The other adds their pages and sends it back. Over time it becomes a shared diary.

### 4. A handwritten "reasons I love you" list

Write it by hand, as long or short as feels true. In research by Kumar and Epley, people who got [thank-you letters reported more surprise and delight](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/people-underestimate-the-power-of-saying-thanks.html) than the writers expected. Writers also overestimated how awkward recipients would feel. So go ahead and say the sappy thing.

### 5. A framed map of both your cities

Mark where each of you lives, or where you met. It's a quiet, everyday reminder that the two dots are connected.

## Everyday reminders that keep showing up

Some gifts aren't a single moment. They're a way to keep reaching each other all year. These are connected gifts: you send from one side, and they receive on the other. Each type works a little differently, so pick the one that fits how you two already like to talk.

|---|---|---|---|
| Touch lamps | A light signal | Both of you | No |
| Touch bracelets | A tap or vibration | Both of you | No |
| Screen message box | Short messages or images on a screen | The receiver | No, it stays on screen |
| Note printer | Notes, doodles and photos printed on paper | The receiver | Yes, a printed note |
| Shared digital photo frame | Photos on a screen | The receiver | No, it stays on screen |

There's research behind small, surprising reach-outs, though it wasn't about couples. Liu and colleagues ran 13 preregistered experiments with nearly 6,000 people. They found [people significantly underestimate how much others appreciate being contacted](https://business.ku.edu/people-underestimate-surprising-impact-reaching-out-study-finds). The reach-outs were things like a brief text or a small gift, sent to friends and acquaintances. And the more surprising the act, the greater the appreciation.

### 6. Touch lamps

A pair of lamps, one for each of you. Tap yours and theirs lights up. It's a wordless "thinking of you" that works well across busy schedules.

### 7. Touch bracelets

These send a tap or a vibration to the other person's wrist. They're subtle and wearable, though they carry a signal rather than words.

## Gifts for the time-zone gap

If you're hours apart, the time difference can feel like a third person in the relationship. These gifts make it a little friendlier.

### 11. A clock set to their time

Put it on your desk or nightstand so you always know if they're eating breakfast or getting into bed. Get them one set to yours, too.

A tip for the clock: daylight saving time doesn't change everywhere on the same day. According to [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division/local-time-faqs), US daylight saving starts on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. Other countries may use different dates, and the Southern Hemisphere changes at opposite times. So your gap can shift by an hour for a few weeks each spring and fall. Check their clock then.

### 12. Notes waiting when they wake up

Schedule an email or message to land at their morning. Or send a printed note before you go to sleep, so it's there when they get up.

### 13. A dual-time watch

It shows two time zones at once. It's a practical gift that quietly says "I keep your hours in mind."

### 14. An "overlap hours" planner

Make a simple chart of the hours you're both awake and free. Frame it or stick it on the fridge so calls stop being a guessing game.

## Shared experiences from afar

Doing the same thing at the same time gives you something new to talk about. It also gives you something other than "how was your day."

That talking matters. In a one-week diary study of dating couples, Jiang and Hancock found long-distance partners [reported feeling more intimate](https://sml.stanford.edu/publications/2013/absence-makes-communication-grow-fonder-geographic-separation-interpersonal-media). The researchers said these couples disclosed more about themselves and idealized their partners' behaviors. Shared experiences give you more to open up about.

### 15. Cook the same recipe

Send them a recipe card plus one special ingredient you can legally ship. Then cook together on a video call and compare results.

### 16. Read the same book

Mail two copies, or send one and keep one. Leave notes in the margins of theirs before it goes out.

### 17. A watch-night kit

Pack their favorite snacks, a cozy blanket and a list of movies to start together. Hit play at the same moment.

### 18. Dinner delivered for a video date

Order a meal from a place near them, timed for your call. You eat your dinner, they eat theirs, and nobody cooks.

### 19. An online class for two

Pick something neither of you knows yet, like drawing, a language or pottery basics. Learning side by side is a fun kind of company.

## Low-budget and DIY ideas

If money's tight, you're in good shape. Flynn and Adams found that givers expected pricier gifts to be more appreciated. But for birthday gifts, [recipients didn't feel greater appreciation for gifts that cost more](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/research-confirms-its-thought-counts). The care you put in is what shows.

### 20. A playlist with liner notes

Make a playlist, then write one line per song about why it's there. The notes are the real gift.

### 21. A folder of voice memos

Record short clips: a goodnight, a silly story, the sound of your street in the morning. They can replay them whenever they want.

### 22. A coupon book

Hand-make coupons for things you'll do on the next visit, or from afar. "One video call where I don't check my phone" counts.

### 23. A photo zine

Fold a few pages, add printed photos, captions and doodles, then staple it. It doesn't need to look polished to mean a lot.

### 24. A hand-drawn comic of your story

Draw how you met, your first visit, or an inside joke. Stick figures are completely fine.

## Comfort for the hard days

Some days the distance just feels heavier. These gifts are for those days, when a little comfort goes a long way.

### 25. A care package

Fill a box with their favorite tea or snacks, a cozy pair of socks, and a short note. Keep it simple and personal.

### 26. A worn hoodie

Send a hoodie or T-shirt you've actually worn. Skip packing a bottle of perfume, though, since USPS prohibits perfumes containing alcohol in mail to all countries (more on shipping below).

### 27. An "open when you miss me" envelope

This one pairs nicely with the open-when letters above. Tuck in a photo, a small trinket and a note about the next time you'll see each other.

## Gifts for the next visit

Looking forward to the next visit can help the wait feel shorter. These gifts turn "someday" into a plan.

### 28. Travel gear

Packing cubes, a comfy neck pillow or a nice toiletry bag for the trip to you. Practical, and a gentle hint that you want them there.

### 29. An adventure fund

Start a jar or savings pot for things you'll do together. Add a note with each contribution about what it's for.

### 30. A planned itinerary

Map out a day of the visit: breakfast spot, a walk, somewhere you've wanted to show them. Print it and send it as a little preview.

## How to send it: shipping tips

If your partner lives in another country, check the rules before you pack. The [USPS shipping restrictions page](https://www.usps.com/international/shipping-restrictions.htm) says international senders must follow USPS rules and the destination country's rules. Look up their country in the USPS Individual Country Listing.

A few common gift items cause trouble:

- **Banned to all countries:** aerosols, alcoholic beverages, nail polish and perfumes containing alcohol. Leave these out of care packages.
- **Lithium batteries:** these can only go internationally when they're installed in the device. Don't send spare batteries loose.
- **Food and other perishables:** these travel at the sender's risk. Choose sturdy, sealed snacks over anything that can spoil.

Sending from outside the US? Your postal service publishes its own list of prohibited items, so check that and the destination country's rules.

Give yourself extra time for international mail and customs, and keep your tracking number handy. If you're unsure about an item, leave it out and send a letter about it instead.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's a good gift for a long-distance relationship?

A good gift keeps you present in their day. Keepsakes like letters and printed photos work well, and so do small everyday reminders and shared plans for your next visit.

### What can I send that's cheap?

Try a playlist with notes, voice memos, a coupon book or a photo zine. In Flynn and Adams' research, recipients didn't appreciate pricier birthday gifts any more than cheaper ones.

### How do I send a gift to another country?

Check both USPS rules and the destination country's rules first. Leave out aerosols, alcohol, nail polish and perfumes containing alcohol, and only send lithium batteries installed in a device.

### What do I send someone I miss?

Something personal and easy to hold onto. Try an "open when you miss me" letter, a worn hoodie, or a handwritten list of reasons you love them.
